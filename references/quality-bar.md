# Quality bar: the famous-site standard

The target is a *level of polish*, the kind well-known sites have: a page that needs no rework to look good. It is not a style to copy. A warm café, a playful event page and a dark developer tool can all meet it in their own direction (design.md). These sites rarely rely on clever effects. Their strength is restraint, applied to every block. The checks below are concrete enough to verify on a screenshot.

## What makes them look good
1. **One clear hierarchy per screen.**
   - There's one main headline. On desktop it is at least 2.5× the body size.
   - A short subline follows, then at most two actions.
   - Nothing competes with the headline.
2. **Space instead of decoration.**
   - Section padding is generous (desktop 80–160 px, mobile 48–80 px) and follows one spacing scale.
   - Text is never squeezed to fit more in.
3. **A quiet palette with one action color.** Neutral backgrounds and text. The accent marks actions and little else, covering roughly 10% of the page or less.
4. **Real media is the hero.**
   - The subject is shown big and sharp: the actual product, place or person.
   - Every image has the same treatment (ratio, corners, color grade).
   - Text over a photo sits on a calm area or a light local scrim, and the photo stays recognizable.
5. **Few, consistent components.**
   - Two button styles (filled primary, plus outline or text secondary) at the same height.
   - One card style.
   - One icon family.
6. **Alignment and rhythm.**
   - One max-width.
   - Edges line up across sections.
   - Headings in the same role have the same size everywhere.
7. **Specific copy.** Short headlines that name the actual thing ("Coffee roasted on the farm, served under the trees"), not generic praise ("Experience the best coffee"). No made-up numbers (content.md).
8. **Motion that serves the content.** Subtle reveals, smooth media. The page reads perfectly with motion off.

## The generic-template look (avoid unless the direction asks for it)
- A sparkle or "new" pill above every hero.
- Glassmorphism stat cards.
- Four icon-in-a-square feature cards.
- The same button repeated on every card.
- Gradient text everywhere.
- A dark hero on an otherwise light page.
- Stock photos of other places.

## Benchmark pass (part of verify)
1. Open 1–2 **live reference websites** from DESIGN.md References (for example the user's reference site or the brand that inspired the direction) at the same width as your screenshot. A font page or a repo is not a benchmark; the gate rejects it.
2. Compare them side by side and write, in the task log, the three biggest differences in hierarchy, spacing, imagery or consistency.
3. Fix those, or record them as known issues with a reason.

"Looks fine" is not a result. Name what you compared.
